## 1. What prompted this

Logan restricted the stimuli for the **new data-collection wave** to 20 of the 39 (`d_optimal_selected.csv`), chosen by D-optimal design (AlgDesign::optFederov in `line_cutting/analysis/stimulus_selection.py`) on four "design factors": Joe Kwon's 2023 universalization score (`univ.agg.welfare`) plus three outcome measures of the one-cutter world (`one.cut.agg.welfare`, `new.first.last.one.cut` = inequality, `ordinal.harm`). So the selection was optimized for **sensitivity to universalization under Joe's model**, which turns out to be essentially L1 shallow (r = .994 across the 39 stimuli). It is blind to where the simulation models disagree with each other.

The Sept-26 pilot of that wave exists (`pilot_Sept_26_{participants,trials}.parquet`): Mexico/UK/US, 276 people after exclusions, 13 main trials each, the 20 stimuli.

Questions: does our story survive on the 20? Should we ask Logan to add scenarios? And, independently, how do we run "the Logan analysis" with our model scores (Sydney's request)?

* Logan's model (`ordinal.py`): ordered-logistic on answers 0–4; per-country cutpoints and slope mean/scale; per-participant intercept (Normal) and slopes (Student-t ν=14), non-centered; **no stimulus random effect**; predictors standardized across trials. NUTS 4 × (2000+2000), target accept .95.

Reconstruction (`make_anon_export.py`): sessions = blocks of 20 consecutive trial ids; repeats = second occurrence within session; 63 ids whose sessions interleave dropped → 1,932 sessions kept, 25,116 main trials. **Stopgap until Logan sends his anonymized export.**
* Final-config simulation runs: all five models 10 runs × 28 maps (L0: 6/280 stuck excluded). Joe's run count unknown (his own 2023 output).
* Model-score correlations across 39 stimuli: Joe–L1h .99, L1h–L2h .99, L1h–realL1 .90, L0–others .73–.76. On the restricted 20 the models agree more (L0–L1h .82, L1h–realL1 .93).

## 4. Interpretation (current best reading)

* Story survives everywhere: shallow best; rollout worse than shallow; L0 worst. "Depth-1 is sufficient" holds under Logan's model.
* Rollout vs L0 on all 39, trial level: **tied** (−1 ± 20). Stronger than the poster's "rollout partly reverses the gains". Needs the paper-grade fit before saying out loud.
* The restricted 20 **mainly costs the shallow-vs-rollout contrast** (per-trial gap drops to a third; the discriminating scenarios are the dropped congested maps). It does *not* rescue L0 under the trial-level model (L0 still −100 on the 20). The "L0 catches up" effect is specific to the stimulus-level univ fit on Joe's 39 means.
* The restricted set is well designed for Logan's question (slopes larger on the 20) and under-powered for ours. Stakes are moderate: the existing 20-country data remains our primary cross-cultural test; the new wave can only be a supplementary replication.
* Joe's score ≡ L1 shallow for all purposes (r .994; ΔELPD within ±15). Useful validation and a sentence for the paper.

## 5. Doubts and open questions (Julien's and Claude's — keep)

1. **`maybe_6` fragility.** Two-thirds of shallow's margin over L0 on moral judgments (trial level) is one scenario, and L0 wins on four others. Needs a leave-one-scenario-out check and an honest robustness paragraph. Is `maybe_6` special (what happens on that map)?
2. **Quick-fit diagnostics.** Full-data fits: 13–37 divergences, R-hat up to 1.09 (Joe) on country-level parameters. Short warm-up. Could the rollout-vs-L0 tie move? Large (4–8 SE) contrasts are unlikely to flip; the tie might.
3. **Reconstructed data.** Session split by blocks of 20, repeats by occurrence, 63 ids dropped, exclusions assumed applied. Replace with Logan's export. Also: N = 2,059 or 1,924 in the paper?
4. **PSIS-LOO reliability.** ~2–3% of trials with Pareto k > 0.7 (per-participant slopes on 13 trials). Consider moment-matching or K-fold for the paper-grade comparison; or report that the ranking is robust to excluding high-k trials.
5. **Slopes are not comparable across conditions** (standardization differs); only within a row. Don't read "effect sizes go up on the 20" as a model result.
6. **Slopes converge on the 20** because the scores correlate more there. Is ELPD the right headline statistic for the paper, or do we need both (slope = effect size, ELPD = comparison)? Current view: both, ELPD for comparison.
7. **No stimulus random effect in Logan's model.** With 4 stimulus-level predictors on 39 stimuli, stimulus-level residual variance is absorbed into the likelihood. A stimulus random intercept would be the conservative check; it also makes the comparison less sensitive to single scenarios (see 1).
8. **Outcome covariates differ** between pipelines: Logan uses Joe's three measures; the poster used Julien's `outcome_metrics.csv` versions (aggregate_welfare, ordinal_harm_blind, inequality). The per-scenario ΔR² figure here uses Joe's, so its totals (+.069, +.027) differ from the poster's (+.113, +.057). Decide which the paper uses and be consistent.
9. **Should L0 be "nerfed" further?** Raised 5 Oct; decided no — L0 is defined on principle (no prediction of others) and must not be tuned to fit worse. A stronger no-prediction baseline, if needed, is a new model with its own definition.
10. **Joe's run count** unknown; the Joe column is his 2023 output. Ask Joe/Logan if it matters.
11. **Idealization/choreography baseline** still not run (pre-journal gap from July). Out of scope for now per Sydney; keep on the list.
12. **Pilot pooled with 20-country data?** Possible as extra "countries" with a different stimulus set; decided to keep separate (different wave/design).
13. **Per-country figure** — the per-country slopes are in every `summary.json`; a forest plot by country for shallow vs L0 vs rollout (like the poster's) hasn't been drawn from the Logan-model fits yet.
14. **US ΔR² of +.113: also mostly `maybe_6`?** Not yet checked with Julien's covariates (needs `joe_moral_agg.csv` + `outcome_metrics.csv`).
